My research interests lie at the intersection of High-Performance Computing (HPC), computer systems, and intelligent resource management. My work focuses on understanding application performance, resource contention, workload characterization, and designing adaptive scheduling and resource-management techniques for parallel and cloud-native applications. I am particularly interested in multicore performance analysis, hardware performance counters, phase-aware workload characterization, interference prediction, and contention-aware scheduling, with the broader goal of improving the efficiency and predictability of HPC systems.

Alongside my research, I have experience as a Graduate Teaching Assistant (GTA) supporting courses in both Electrical Engineering & Computer Science and Mechanical Engineering at the University of Kansas. Teaching and mentoring students have strengthened my interest in making complex computing concepts approachable, and I have also worked with high-school students through KU engineering summer programs, helping introduce them to engineering and computing.

Publications

Assessing Processor Allocation Strategies for Online Scheduling of Moldable Task Graphs

Mary Jeevana Pudota, Krishna Chaitanya Reddy Chitta, Sai Rithvik Gundla, Hongyang Sun

2025 IEEE 32nd International Conference on High Performance Computing, Data, and Analytics (HiPC), pp. 119–129, December 2025

Abstract

In online scheduling of moldable task graphs, each task can run on a varying number of processors and is only revealed once its predecessors finish, so processor allocation must be decided without knowing future tasks. Prior work analyzed allocation algorithms theoretically through competitive ratios; this paper evaluates how those strategies actually perform in practice and how their fixed parameter settings affect makespan.

A Dynamic Resource Management Framework and Reconfiguration Strategies for Cloud-native Bulk Synchronous Parallel Applications

Krishna Chaitanya Reddy Chitta

M.S. Thesis, The University of Kansas

Abstract

BSP applications are increasingly deployed on shared, cloud-native platforms such as Kubernetes, where resource variability and workload imbalance across supersteps cause stragglers and longer execution times. Existing approaches use one static configuration for the whole run. This thesis builds a dynamic resource management framework and evaluates reconfiguration strategies that adapt resources as the workload changes.
